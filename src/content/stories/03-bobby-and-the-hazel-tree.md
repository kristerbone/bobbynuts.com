The meadow was long and golden. Bobby pattered through the grass, past the old stone wall, and there he met Hattie Hedgehog.

"Lovely day for a walk," said Hattie, who always spoke in rhymes. "Where are you off to? Tell me, do!"

"I'm going to the house with the hazel trees," said Bobby.

"Then you're nearly there, my little friend! Just past the gate, and round the bend!"

Bobby's heart went *thump, thump, thump*. He ran. He leaped over the wall. He squeezed under the gate.

And there it was.

The house stood warm and quiet, with a golden light in the window. All around it, the garden was full of trees. And in the middle, the biggest of all, stood a hazel tree. Its branches were heavy with nuts.

"Nuts and acorns," breathed Bobby. "Hazelnuts!"

He scampered up the trunk. He picked one nut, and then another. They tasted just like sunshine.

But just then a loud voice screeched, "Oi! Those are MY nuts!"

A big, noisy bird swooped down. It was Mr Jay, with his blue-and-black wings and a very grumpy beak.

"Go away, squirrel!" said Mr Jay. "I've been guarding this tree all autumn!"

Bobby wanted to say something grumpy back. But then he saw Mr Jay's face more closely. Under all the shouting, the bird looked rather sad.

"Do you live here all by yourself?" asked Bobby gently.

Mr Jay blinked. "...Yes," he said, in a much smaller voice. "Nobody ever visits."

Bobby thought about his long journey. He thought about Pip, and Old Mossy, and Hattie. He thought about how nice it was to have friends.

"Tail up," he said softly. "I've got an idea. This tree has far more nuts than one jay could ever eat. What if we shared them? And I could help you look after the tree."

Mr Jay's beak fell open. "You would do that?"

"Of course," said Bobby. "That's what friends are for."

So Bobby and Mr Jay picked nuts together until the sun began to set. Pip the Wren arrived, and Hattie came along too, and they all sat on the warm branches and shared a hazelnut feast.

Then Bobby found a snug hollow in the hazel tree, just the right size for a little red squirrel. He curled up in it with his bushy tail over his nose.

Far above, at the top of the valley, the tall pine tree creaked in the wind. But Bobby wasn't cold or hungry or alone any more.

Down in the garden, a light shone in the window.

Bobby Nut was home.
