Bobby and Pip the Wren went down through the Bluebell Wood. It was cool and shady, and the ground was as blue as the sky.

Bobby hopped over roots. He scampered under ferns. He found a tiny nut and ate it in two nibbles. Then he heard a sound.

*Splash! Gurgle! Rush!*

"What's that noise?" asked Bobby.

"The stream," said Pip. "We have to cross it to get to the meadow."

They came out of the trees, and there it was. The stream was wide and fast and full of white bubbles. Across it lay one long, old log. It was green with moss and it looked very, very wobbly.

Bobby put one paw on the log. It wobbled. He put another paw on it. It wobbled some more.

"I can't do it," said Bobby, and his tail drooped. "What if I fall in?"

"Hmm," said Pip. "I can fly across, but you can't."

Just then, a gruff voice came from under a bush. "Evening, little ones."

It was Old Mossy the Badger, who was slow and wise and smelled a little like wet leaves.

"I can't cross the bridge," said Bobby. "It's too wobbly."

Old Mossy sat down and thought for a long time. Then he said, "Every wobbly thing needs two things. Something to hold on to, and a friend to help."

"Something to hold on to?" said Bobby.

Bobby looked up. Above the log hung a long, bendy branch that stretched all the way across the stream. It was a bit like a handrail.

"Tail up!" cried Bobby. "I've got an idea!"

Pip flew up and tied a long piece of ivy to the branch, so that it hung down beside the log. Old Mossy leaned his big, heavy body on one end of the log, to keep it from rolling.

Bobby took a deep breath. He held on to the ivy with his front paws. He put one back paw on the log, then the other. *Wobble, wobble.* He took a step. *Wobble, wobble, wobble.* Another step.

"You're doing it!" cried Pip.

"Don't look down!" called Old Mossy.

Bobby looked straight ahead. He took one step, and one more, and one more, until at last his paws touched the soft grass on the other side.

"I did it!" he shouted. "We did it!"

Old Mossy gave a slow, proud smile. "Remember, Bobby Nut. You don't have to be brave all by yourself."

Bobby gave him a big squirrel hug, as high as he could reach, which was about up to the badger's knee.

Then, with a happy flick of his tail, he set off across the meadow.
