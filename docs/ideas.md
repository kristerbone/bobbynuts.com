## Sharing and balance (Jen, October 2026)

Make sharing and balance part of the message in both the current 12 Stories and the older series.

- **Take what you need, leave some for others.** When Bobby Nuts finds food, he learns to leave enough for the next hungry animal.
- **The Nut Box mystery.** The ending could be about sharing: the Nut Box Squirrel isn't a thief, and the two squirrels learn there's enough for both.
- **Bouncy and still.** Bobby Nuts's arc becomes balance: knowing when to bounce and when to be still, rather than learning to stop bouncing. His bounciness is one of his gifts.
- **Giving back.** Squirrels bury nuts for winter and forget some, and those can grow into trees, so even a forgetful squirrel helps the wood. (Check this holds for red squirrels before using it.)
- **The wood in balance (older series).** The trees and fungi trade food underground, and a wild wood is a balance of young and old trees, living and dead wood, and eaters and eaten. People can help keep that balance, or upset it.

## Kindness, gratitude and not worrying (Jen, October 2026)

Weave positive themes through the first series. Show them in what the characters do, never as a lecture.

- **Kindness.** Small kind acts move the story on: a friend shows him the way, shares a meal or keeps watch. Bobby Nuts passes kindness on to the next friend, so it travels down Good Wood with him.
- **Gratitude.** A small bedtime habit: each night in his drey, Bobby Nuts remembers good things from his day. Children could join in, and it could become a family routine.
- **Not worrying.** Bobby Nuts starts hungry, alone and worried about the long way down. He learns gentle ways to cope:
  - **Being still.** Stopping, breathing slowly and listening calms him down. The series's main skill doubles as a way to calm worry.
  - **One step at a time.** He doesn't have to manage the whole journey, only the next stretch of the Burn.
  - **Asking for help.** Every time he's worried, a friend is there. Worries shrink when you share them.
  - **Most worries don't come true.** The buzzard he fears turns out to be friendly, and so do the cats.
- **Ending safe and warm.** Already a rule in the guide, and it backs up the message that things usually turn out all right.

## Nature series for older readers (Jen, October 2026)

A series that teaches real biology, history and nature while telling a good story. Every fact is accurate and checked.

### Who it's for

- Children of about 9 and up, so Eva's age and older, growing with them as they get older.
- Parents too: just as informative for the grown-up reading along as for the child.
- This is a different reader from the current 12-Story plan (ages 3 to 7). Still to decide: is this a separate series, or does the whole series move older?

### First series: how a wood works

- **Mycelium networks**: how trees and fungi are connected underground and share food and warnings. Fits the series theme that everyone helps each other.
- **Wild wood or planted wood**: how to tell a wild, naturally growing wood from a farmed plantation. Mixed ages and kinds of tree, dead wood, fungi and a messy floor, compared with neat rows of one kind of tree. Avoid calling plantations "bad": red squirrels do well in Scottish conifer plantations, partly because grey squirrels don't.
- **People and nature over time**: how people have changed woods and the land, from early humans to now. Told honestly but with hope: what was lost, who is helping it come back, and what readers can do.
- **What to look out for**: things readers can spot on their own walks, perhaps as a field-notes page at the end of each chapter.

### Parallels for parents

- The family have just got a shared puppy. Raising a puppy and raising a child have a lot in common (patience, staying calm, routine, letting them explore safely), and that could give the parents something too.
- This links to the series theme of being still: patience is what lets you hear the animals, and it's also what a puppy, or a child, needs from you.

### Open questions

- Who tells the story at this age? Bobby Nuts, an older narrator, or a family member?
- Format: longer chapters (perhaps 1,500 to 3,000 words) for confident readers, with facts in the story and fuller notes at the back.
